Everyone shares locations as Google Maps links, and on an iPhone those links keep landing in Safari — a slow web page with a banner asking you to install an app, when Apple Maps was one tap away the whole time. It is a small friction that happens several times a week.

This guide covers how map links actually work on Apple devices: why they open where they do, how to route them somewhere better, what each mapping service collects about you, and the browser tools that make travel planning less tedious.

## Why Map Links Open in the Browser
iOS opens a link in an app only when that app has claimed the exact domain through universal links. Google's regional domains, shortened `goo.gl` URLs and redirect chains all break that chain, which is why the same kind of link behaves inconsistently.

## Opening Google Maps Links in Apple Maps
A shared pin, an address card or a full set of directions can be handed to Apple Maps directly, keeping the destination and the route intact. No copying addresses between apps and no detour through a mobile web page.

## Changing the Default Maps App
iOS lets you set a default browser and mail client, but maps work differently. What you can and cannot change on iPhone and iPad, and the practical workarounds for the parts Apple has not opened up.

## Apple Maps vs Google Maps on Privacy
The two services differ substantially in what they log, how they tie location history to an account, and how long that data persists. Worth understanding before deciding which one gets your daily commute.

## URL Schemes and Sharing Locations Between Apps
Apple Maps supports a documented URL scheme that other apps and shortcuts can build links against — useful for automations, itineraries and anything that needs to hand a destination to another tool.

## Safari Extensions for Travel
Currency conversion while booking, translating listings and signage, copying addresses without fighting the selection handles, and keeping an itinerary reachable from the browser.
